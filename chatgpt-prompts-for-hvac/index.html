ChatGPT prompts for an HVAC business: 14 you can copy today

ChatGPT prompts for an HVAC business: 14 copy-ready prompts for customer messages, job summaries, reminders, job posts, social captions, SOPs and review replies.

Blake RuwaliOwner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo5 min read · Nov 14, 2026

These 14 ChatGPT prompts for an HVAC business cover customer messages, job summaries, maintenance reminders, job posts, social captions, SOPs and review replies. Copy one, replace everything in brackets with your own details, and read the result before using it. They work because they ask ChatGPT to handle words, not facts. Never paste customer names, addresses or phone numbers, and never ask it for prices, codes or diagnoses.

I run an HVAC company on Long Island and in New York City, and I use tools like this for writing. I explain the ground rules in ChatGPT for contractors: do's and don'ts. Here is the short version.

Three rules before you paste anything

  1. No customer PII. No names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, payment details or photos with identifying details. Write "the customer" and "the home" instead.
  2. No prices, codes or diagnoses. It doesn't know your costs, your local rules or what's wrong with a system. You supply those, or you leave them out.
  3. You read it before it goes anywhere. It adds promises you never made. Delete them.

A tip that helps every prompt: paste two short examples of how you write, and ask it to match the tone. It will sound more like you.

Customer messages

1. Turn rough notes into a text

You write for a small HVAC company. Rewrite this note as a friendly text message under 60 words, plain language, no promises about price or arrival time. Note: [your rough note, no names or addresses]

2. Running-late message

Write a short, polite text telling a customer our technician is running about [number] minutes behind. Apologize once, don't give a reason that isn't true, and don't promise anything else. Match this tone: [paste one of your own short texts]

3. Follow-up after an unsold estimate

Write two short follow-up messages for a customer who got an estimate for [type of work, like a system replacement] and hasn't replied. The first goes out on day 2 and the second on day 7. Friendly, no pressure, no discounts, no deadlines I haven't mentioned. Offer to answer questions. Keep each under 50 words.

Job summaries

4. Tech notes to an invoice summary

Turn these tech notes into a clear three-sentence job summary for an invoice. Use only the facts below. If something is missing, write "not noted" instead of guessing. Notes: [paste notes, no names or addresses]

5. Notes to a handoff for the next tech

From these notes, write a short handoff for the next technician: what was done, what was recommended, and what the customer declined. Use only what's in the notes and mark anything unclear as "unclear." Notes: [paste notes]

A tech should read and correct these. The note becomes the record.

Maintenance reminders

6. Seasonal tune-up reminder

Write a short, friendly reminder text for customers on our maintenance plan that it's time to schedule their [spring or fall] tune-up. Under 50 words, no price, no promises about dates, and invite them to reply or call [your office number].

7. Plan renewal notice

Write a clear, polite email reminding a maintenance plan member that their plan renews on [date]. Don't quote a price. Say what stays the same and tell them how to reach us with questions. Keep it under 120 words.

Job posts

8. Technician job post

Write a job post for an HVAC technician at a small company serving [your area]. Include the day-to-day work, the kind of equipment they'll see, and what we're looking for. Don't mention pay, benefits or schedule details. I'll add those. Keep it plain and honest, no hype.

9. Interview questions

Give me 10 interview questions for an HVAC technician candidate that cover troubleshooting approach, working with customers, and safety habits. Add what a good answer tends to include for each.

Social captions

10. Photo caption for a finished job

Write three short social media captions for a photo of a finished [type of job]. Friendly and plain. No claims about savings, no prices, no promises, and don't include the customer's name or address. Add one line asking people to message us with questions.

11. Seasonal tip post

Write a short social post with one practical, general tip for homeowners about [topic, like changing filters]. Plain language, under 80 words, no claims about savings, and end by pointing them to our contact page.

SOPs

12. Turn a process into a checklist

I'll describe how we do [a routine task, like opening the office or closing out a job]. Turn it into a numbered checklist with one action per line. Don't add steps I didn't mention. At the end, list questions about anything that seems missing. My description: [your description]

13. First-week plan for a new hire

I'm onboarding a new HVAC apprentice. Draft a 5-day plan with learning goals per day, shadowing tasks and a short quiz each afternoon. Ask me three questions first about what our shop does.

Review replies

14. Reply to a Google review

Write a short, warm reply to this customer review. Thank them, mention one specific thing they said, and don't repeat private details or promise anything. If the review is negative, stay calm, apologize for the experience, and invite them to call [your office number]. Review: [paste the review text only, no names]

For the bigger picture on reviews, see AI and Google reviews.

Where ChatGPT stops

These prompts save time on words. They don't connect to your phones, your price book or your schedule. If you want a phone agent that creates jobs, or estimate drafts from your own pricing, that takes tools connected to your systems. I cover where I'd start in AI tools for HVAC owners.

FAQ

What are the best ChatGPT prompts for an HVAC business? The ones that turn your facts into clean writing: customer texts, job summaries, reminders, job posts, social captions, checklists and review replies. Give it the facts and the tone, and keep prices, codes and diagnoses out.

Is it safe to paste customer information into ChatGPT? I wouldn't. Remove names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and payment details first, and check the privacy settings of whatever tool you use. If you wouldn't put it on a postcard, don't paste it.

Can ChatGPT write HVAC quotes? It shouldn't. It doesn't know your labor rate, your equipment costs or your overhead, and it will give you a confident number anyway. Pricing needs your own price book and a person checking it.

Can I use ChatGPT to diagnose a system or check code? No. It can be wrong, outdated or specific to the wrong place. Use the manufacturer's manual, your supplier or your local authority, and use ChatGPT only to help you word the question.

How do I make the replies sound like me? Paste two short examples of your own writing and ask it to match the tone. Then edit the result, because you are the one who sends it.
