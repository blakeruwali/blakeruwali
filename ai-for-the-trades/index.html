AI for the trades: what it actually does in a real HVAC company

AI for the trades, from an HVAC owner who builds it: the five jobs it does in a real shop, what still needs a person, and where to start.

Blake RuwaliOwner of Home+ Air & Heat (Long Island) and founder of Techeo4 min read · Nov 14, 2026

AI for the trades means using software that can read, write, talk and sort information to take on the paperwork and phone work around the trade: answering calls, writing estimates from photos, following up, scheduling and measuring. It does not turn a wrench, and it does not replace the tech in the house. In my HVAC company it handles the work that used to pile up in the office, and a person checks anything that touches a customer's money or safety.

I'm Blake. I run Home+ Air & Heat on Long Island, and I build the AI that runs it. Most of what you read about AI for contractors is written by people who sell software. This is what it looks like inside a shop with trucks, techs and a phone that never stops.

The five jobs AI does in our shop

1. It answers the phone when we can't. A customer calling about a dead system at 9 pm doesn't leave a voicemail anymore, they call the next company. We use an AI phone agent for calls the office can't pick up. It collects the problem, the address and the best number, and gets the job into our system for a person to follow up. It does not diagnose over the phone and it does not quote prices it isn't sure about. Details are in how to answer every missed call.

2. It drafts the estimate from the photos. A tech takes photos of the equipment, the nameplate and the space. Our estimate copilot reads them, proposes the equipment and line items from our own price book, and builds a draft proposal. A person reviews it before the customer sees it. The AI saves the typing and the lookup, not the judgment. More in AI estimates with a human check.

3. It makes the customer's yes easy. Proposals and memberships go out as a link, and the customer reviews and signs on a phone. No printing, no "let me email you a PDF."

4. It books with real availability. Online booking shows real open slots from the schedule instead of "we'll call you back." It sits on top of ServiceTitan, which is where our jobs already live.

5. It follows up so the office doesn't have to chase. Unsold estimates, maintenance reminders, rebate paperwork nudges. Campaigns run on a schedule, and a person steps in when a customer replies with a real question.

One more tool that isn't about the office: we use an iPad with LiDAR to scan a house, turn the scan into a floor plan, and feed it into a Manual J load calculation. Sizing equipment by measurement beats sizing by rule of thumb.

What AI does not do

  • It doesn't replace a tech. Installing, diagnosing and fixing are still hands-on.
  • It doesn't make the final call on price, scope or safety. A person does.
  • It doesn't fix a broken process. If your office has no follow-up habit, AI will automate the absence of one.
  • It doesn't always get it right. We check the work, and we keep the AI away from anything it can't verify.

Why a real shop matters

Every tool we build is used at Home+ first. If the phone agent confuses a customer, I hear about it from my own office. If the estimate copilot picks the wrong part, a tech tells me on the truck. That feedback loop is the reason I trust what I share, and the reason I'll tell you what failed. I started this site because I wanted contractors to hear from someone who has to live with the result.

Where to start (in this order)

  1. Missed calls. It's the cheapest leak to plug and easiest to see. Count the calls you didn't answer last week.
  2. Estimate follow-up. Pick one rule: every unsold estimate gets a message at day 2 and day 7 written in your voice.
  3. Estimate drafting. Only after your price book is clean. AI can't price from a messy book.
  4. Scheduling and booking. Once the first three are stable.

Skip anything that needs you to rebuild your whole business first. Start with one workflow, run it for a month and write down what went wrong.

FAQ

What is AI for the trades? It's using AI tools for the work around the trade: phones, estimates, follow-up, scheduling, measuring and marketing. The hands-on work stays with the tech.

Can AI replace HVAC technicians or plumbers? Not the hands-on work. AI can't climb into a crawlspace or braze a joint. It can take over parts of the office workload, and techs who use it will have less paperwork.

What should a small trades business try first? Missed-call handling, because the leak is easy to see and the setup is small. Then estimate follow-up.

Do I need ServiceTitan to use AI in my shop? No. We build on ServiceTitan because that's where our jobs live. Whatever system you use, the AI needs access to your real schedule, price book and customer history to be useful.

Is it safe to let AI talk to my customers? It can be, if you limit what it can say, keep it from quoting prices it can't verify, and have a person review anything involving money, scope or safety.
