AI for the trades

Is AI going to replace HVAC techs? My honest answer

Is AI going to replace HVAC techs? Short answer: not the hands-on work. Here's what AI changes for techs and trade jobs, from an HVAC company owner.

Blake RuwaliOwner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo4 min read · Nov 14, 2026

No, AI is not going to replace HVAC techs for the hands-on work. It can't braze a line set, pull a coil, set a condenser or read what a system is doing in a crawl space. What it does is take over paperwork around the job: notes, estimates, follow-up and call intake. I run an HVAC company and build the AI we use, so I see this from both sides. Here's what I think changes and what doesn't.

What AI can't do

The core of the trade is physical and judgment-heavy.

  • The work itself. Installing, repairing and tuning equipment in real homes with tight spaces and surprises.
  • Reading the situation. A tech walks in and notices the thing nobody said: the clogged return, the wrong wire, the unit that doesn't match the house.
  • Safety calls. Gas, combustion and electrical decisions need a trained person on site.
  • Trust. A customer lets a tech into the house. That relationship isn't software.

I can't predict the far future, and neither can anyone selling you a prediction. What I can tell you is what I see in an actual company today: AI is a tool the people use, not a replacement for them.

What AI does at Home+

Here's the actual list of what we use it for:

  • An AI phone agent for calls the office can't answer
  • An estimate copilot that drafts proposals from tech photos using our price book, with a person checking every one
  • Proposals and memberships signed on a phone
  • Online booking with real slots
  • Follow-up campaigns
  • iPad LiDAR scans that become a floor plan and a Manual J load calculation

Notice what's on the list: calls, paperwork, scheduling, sales support and measuring. None of it turns a wrench. For the full picture, see AI for HVAC contractors.

What changes for techs

Some parts of the job do shift.

  1. Photos matter more. Good photos feed the estimate copilot, so a tech who documents the job well makes the whole process better. I explain the flow in AI estimates.
  2. Less typing. Notes and write-ups can be drafted from what the tech says, then corrected.
  3. Measuring gets faster. A LiDAR scan can replace a lot of tape-measure work. See LiDAR and Manual J.
  4. Checking the AI becomes a skill. A person reviews every estimate that touches money or scope. A tech who knows what a correct proposal looks like is the one who catches the wrong one.
  5. Customers get quicker answers. The office isn't the bottleneck.

What doesn't change

Training, tools, a deep knowledge of how systems work and being someone customers trust. If anything, the techs I'd worry least about are the ones who understand the equipment deeply, because they can tell when a draft is wrong.

The jobs most exposed

Here's my honest view. The work most likely to be reshaped is the repetitive desk work: data entry, typing up notes, chasing unsigned proposals, basic call intake. Those jobs don't vanish overnight, but their shape changes. I don't have numbers on this, and I'd distrust anyone who gives you a confident percentage. If you run a shop, the practical move is to teach people to use the tools rather than fear them.

What a tech can do this month

  • Take clearer, more complete photos on every job
  • Learn what the estimate copilot or your price book includes, so you can spot a missing line
  • Try ChatGPT for a plain-language customer explanation, and read my notes on what to do and not do with ChatGPT
  • Ask your owner how AI is being used and where a human check applies

What owners should do

Be clear about where AI is allowed to act and where a person decides. In our shop, anything about price, scope or safety gets checked by a person. Tell your team that. People worry less when they know the rule. If you're picking tools, these questions help, and the broader overview is AI for the trades.

My closing thought

Here's how I'd put it: techs who use AI will replace techs who don't. That's an opinion, not a fact, and it applies to the paperwork and the planning around the job. The work in the house is still yours.

FAQ

Is AI going to replace HVAC techs? Not for the hands-on work. AI can handle calls, drafts and paperwork, but a person still installs, repairs and makes the safety calls in the home.

Will AI take trade jobs? It will change parts of them, mostly the repetitive office tasks. I can't promise how any job will look years from now, and I'd be skeptical of anyone who does.

What should an HVAC tech learn about AI? How to take good photos, how to review an AI draft for mistakes and how your company's price book works. Knowing the equipment well is what lets you catch a wrong estimate.

Do HVAC companies use AI to replace staff? At Home+ we use it for tasks like unanswered calls, drafting estimates and follow-up. A person reviews anything touching money, scope or safety. That's how I run it.

What parts of an HVAC job are safest from AI? Installation, repair, tuning, combustion and electrical work, and being on site with a customer. Those are physical and judgment-based.
