# AI estimates for contractors: photos to proposal, with a human check

**Meta description:** How AI turns job photos into a draft contractor estimate, what it needs to work, where it goes wrong, and why a person must check before the customer sees it.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-estimates/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

AI can draft a contractor estimate from job photos by reading the equipment and the space, matching them to line items in your own price book, and assembling a proposal. It can't be trusted to send one alone. The safe version is: the tech takes the photos, the AI drafts, a person checks every line, and only then does the customer see it. That's how we do it at Home+ Air & Heat, and it's what I'd demand from any tool.

## What the workflow looks like

1. **A tech takes photos.** Nameplate on the existing unit, the unit itself, the install location, panel, ductwork or lineset runs, anything unusual.
2. **The AI reads them.** It identifies the make, model and age when the nameplate is legible, notes the space and access, and flags what it can't tell.
3. **It proposes line items from your price book.** Not from the internet, not from guesses. If the part isn't in your book, it should say so rather than invent one.
4. **It drafts the proposal.** Options, descriptions in plain language, the tech's notes.
5. **A person checks.** The estimator or owner reviews every line, quantity and price, fixes what's wrong, and approves.
6. **The customer gets a link.** They review on their phone and sign there.

The AI saves the typing, the lookups and the formatting. It doesn't save you from knowing your trade.

## What the AI needs to work

- **A clean price book.** If your items are duplicated, mis-priced or described as "misc," the AI will copy the mess. Fix the book first. This is the unglamorous step that matters most.
- **Good photos.** Readable nameplates, light, distance. A tech can learn to take the right six photos in a few minutes.
- **A short checklist the tech follows.** The AI can only see what was photographed.
- **Your rules in writing.** Which brands you sell, which scopes you include, what you never quote without a site visit.

## Where it goes wrong (and what to check)

- **Misread nameplates.** A blurred or partial model number leads to the wrong equipment. Check every model number against the photo.
- **Wrong scope.** It might miss a condensate pump, a pad, a line set replacement or a permit because the photo didn't show the need. Check the job against what the tech actually saw.
- **Pricing from stale data.** If your costs changed, the draft will be wrong. Review prices against current distributor costs.
- **Confident wording.** AI drafts read well even when they're wrong. Read each line as if a stranger wrote it.
- **Linked items.** In software like ServiceTitan, one line can pull other lines with it. Check the total after changes, not just the line you edited.

## The rule: a person approves every estimate

I treat AI as a fast junior estimator who never gets tired and sometimes gets it wrong. The final number is a decision about my company's money and a customer's budget. A person owns it. I don't share results or promise customers anything based on an AI draft alone, and I don't claim savings from it.

## Proposals people can actually say yes to

A good estimate isn't just correct, it's easy to read. Our proposals go out as a link, show good/better/best options in plain language, and the customer can sign and choose a membership on a phone. Fewer steps between "that looks right" and "yes." Pair the draft with fast follow-up: a message within a day or two to any unsold estimate, written in your voice.

## What to ask a vendor selling AI estimates

- Show me a real estimate built from real photos, including one it got wrong.
- Does it quote from my price book only?
- Who reviews before it goes to the customer?
- What happens when it isn't sure?
- Can I see what it changed after a human edit?

## Where to start

Pick your most common job (for us, a straightforward system replacement). Have a tech photograph three of them with the checklist. Draft estimates with AI, check them against how your estimator would have priced them by hand, and write down every difference. After ten jobs you'll know whether it fits your shop.

## FAQ

**Can AI write an HVAC estimate from photos?**
It can draft one: identify equipment from nameplates, propose line items from your price book and assemble a proposal. A person should check every line before the customer sees it.

**Is AI accurate enough to price a job?**
Not on its own. It's fast, but it can misread photos, miss scope and use stale pricing. Treat its output as a draft.

**Do I need a clean price book first?**
Yes. AI can only choose from items it can find. Duplicates, missing descriptions and outdated costs produce bad drafts.

**Can ChatGPT do this by itself?**
ChatGPT can help write descriptions or summarize notes, but it doesn't know your price book, parts, or labor unless you give it that information, and it can invent plausible numbers. A purpose-built workflow connected to your data is safer.

**Should I tell customers an AI helped?**
It's good practice to be honest if asked. What matters most is that a named person at your company stands behind the estimate.
