# AI follow-up for unsold HVAC estimates: a day-2, day-7 cadence

**Meta description:** AI follow-up for unsold HVAC estimates: a day-2 and day-7 cadence in the owner's voice, when to stop, how a person handles replies, and what not to automate.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-estimate-follow-up/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

AI follow-up for unsold HVAC estimates means a short, automatic sequence of messages that goes out after you send a proposal, sounds like the owner, and stops the moment the customer replies, books or says no. A simple cadence is a note on day 2, a check-in on day 7 and a last message a couple of weeks later. A person handles every reply, and nothing about price or scope is decided by the AI.

I run Home+ Air & Heat, and follow-up campaigns are part of how we work. This is the cadence I'd use and the rules around it. It builds on [AI estimates: from photos to proposal](/ai-estimates/).

## Why follow-up matters

A proposal that gets no answer isn't a no. Often the customer is busy, comparing, or waiting for a spouse. Nobody on your end called back, and the quote slid down the list. Follow-up fixes the part that's a process problem. It doesn't fix a price that's wrong for the customer, and it shouldn't try.

## The cadence

Here's a starting point. Adjust the timing to how your customers decide.

1. **Day 2: thank and offer help.** Short. Say the proposal is in their inbox, ask if they have questions.
2. **Day 7: check in.** Ask whether they'd like to talk through the options or change anything.
3. **Day 14: last friendly note.** Say you'll leave it with them and they can reach you anytime.

Three messages is enough. More reads as pressure.

## Write it in the owner's voice

The AI drafts, but you write the voice. Give it three or four real messages you've sent, then edit what it produces. Here's an example of the tone I mean, for day 2:

> Hi, it's Blake from Home+. Just making sure the proposal came through. If any part of it is unclear, reply here and I'll go over it with you.

Day 7:

> Checking in on the proposal I sent last week. Happy to walk through the options or adjust anything. Just reply and I'll call you.

Day 14:

> I'll leave the proposal with you. If anything changes, reply here and I'll pick it up.

Notice what's missing: discounts, deadlines, invented urgency and any claim about prices going up. Don't let the AI add those.

## Stop rules

Write these before you turn anything on.

- **Stop on any reply.** Even "thanks." A person takes over.
- **Stop when they book or sign.** Pull them out of the sequence immediately.
- **Stop on "no" or "stop."** Honor opt-outs right away.
- **Stop if there's an open service issue.** A customer waiting on a repair shouldn't get a sales nudge.
- **Stop at three messages.** Then they go on a long-term list, not a pestering loop.
- **Check text and email consent rules** for your state, and have your attorney review your wording.

## Human reply handling

This is the part to get right. When someone replies, a person answers, as soon as they can. The AI can help by summarizing the thread and suggesting a reply, but the person sends it.

Replies tend to fall into a few groups:

- **Questions:** a person answers, using the proposal.
- **Price or scope concerns:** a person handles these. Never the AI.
- **"Let me think":** thank them and note a date to check back.
- **"We went with someone else":** thank them, ask nothing more.
- **Angry or confused:** a person calls.

## What not to automate

- Price changes, discounts or financing terms
- Anything that changes the scope of work
- Safety statements about the equipment in their home
- Answers to warranty or rebate questions
- Pressure tactics of any kind

A person checks anything touching money, scope or safety. Follow-up is a nudge, not a negotiation.

## How to set it up

1. List your open proposals and how old they are.
2. Pick the cadence and write the three messages yourself.
3. Connect the sequence to your job system so it knows who signed. We keep our jobs in ServiceTitan.
4. Start with a small batch of recent estimates.
5. Read every reply for the first two weeks.
6. Count how many open proposals got a response, not just how many messages went out.

## FAQ

**How often should I follow up on an HVAC estimate?**
A reasonable start is day 2, day 7 and day 14, then stop. Three light messages respect the customer and keep the quote from going cold.

**Can AI write the follow-up messages?**
Yes, as a draft in your voice. Review the wording first, and don't let it add discounts, deadlines or urgency you haven't approved.

**When should follow-up stop?**
When the customer replies, books, signs, opts out or reaches the end of the cadence. Stop immediately if there's an open service problem.

**Who should answer replies?**
A person. The AI can summarize the thread and suggest a reply, but pricing, scope and safety questions need a human.

**Is it okay to text customers a follow-up?**
Rules on texting and email consent vary. Check your state's requirements and have your attorney review your wording before you send.
