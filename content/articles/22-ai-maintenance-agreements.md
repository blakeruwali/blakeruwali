# AI for HVAC maintenance agreement renewals and reminders

**Meta description:** AI for HVAC maintenance agreement renewals and reminders: a simple reminder cadence, how to get the visit booked, what to keep human, and how to handle text consent.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-maintenance-agreements/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

AI can handle the repetitive part of HVAC maintenance agreements: reminding members that a visit is due, helping them book it and nudging them before the agreement renews. A person should handle everything else, including cancellations, complaints and billing questions. Set the reminders on a fixed schedule, write them in your own voice, get consent before texting, and stop messaging the moment a customer replies with a problem.

I run Home+ Air & Heat, where memberships are signed on a phone and jobs live in ServiceTitan, so this is a process I care about. It's the same idea as [AI follow-up for unsold estimates](/ai-estimate-follow-up/), applied to customers you already have.

## Why this work gets missed

Nobody forgets a membership on purpose. A tech is on a job, the office is busy, and the list of customers due for a visit sits there. The customer assumes you'll call. You assume they'll call. The visit doesn't happen, and then the agreement is hard to renew because the customer never saw a reason to.

That's a process problem, which makes it a good fit for automation. The goal is simple: every member hears from you at the right time, and every reply reaches a person.

## A reminder cadence to start from

Adjust the timing to how your agreements and seasons work.

1. **Welcome message after signup.** Say what the agreement includes, when to expect the first visit and how to reach you. Use the real terms from your agreement, not a paraphrase.
2. **Visit due, first notice.** A few weeks before the visit is due, invite them to pick a time.
3. **Visit due, second notice.** About a week later if they haven't booked. Offer a link or ask them to reply.
4. **Visit due, last nudge.** A final short message. Then pass the customer to the office for a call.
5. **After the visit.** Thank them, confirm what was done using the tech's notes and mention when the next visit is due.
6. **Before renewal.** Send a heads-up well ahead of the renewal date, then a short reminder closer to it.

Four or five messages a year per member is plenty. More reads as noise.

## Booking the visit

A reminder without an easy next step just creates a call. Give the customer a way to pick a time without waiting on hold. That can be a booking link with real open slots, which I describe in [AI online booking](/ai-online-booking/), or a reply the office answers quickly. If your system can create the appointment directly in ServiceTitan or whatever you use, the customer gets a confirmation and the office doesn't retype anything.

## Write in your voice

Give the AI three or four real messages you've sent, then edit its drafts. Here's the tone I mean for a visit reminder:

> Hi, it's Blake from Home+. Your system is due for its maintenance visit. You can pick a time here, or reply and we'll set it up for you.

And a renewal heads-up:

> Your maintenance agreement renews soon. If you'd like to go over it or change anything, reply here and we'll call you.

Leave out threats, countdowns and invented urgency. Don't let the AI add prices, discounts or promises about what a visit will find.

## What to keep human

- **Cancellations.** Someone who wants out deserves a person, not another automated nudge. Stop all reminders for that customer immediately and let the office call.
- **Complaints.** If a reply mentions a problem, a missed visit or frustration, route it to a manager as soon as it arrives.
- **Billing questions.** Charges, refunds, card problems and disputed amounts go to a person who can see the account.
- **Equipment problems.** If a member says the system isn't working, that's a service call, not a reminder thread.
- **Anything about price or terms.** The AI can point to your agreement. It shouldn't explain or change it.

A simple rule: the AI sends and books, and any reply that isn't "yes, that time works" goes to a person.

## Consent for texts

Texting customers is regulated. In general, you need the customer's permission to send them automated marketing or reminder texts, and every message needs an easy way to opt out, such as replying STOP. Ask for consent when they sign the agreement, record that they said yes and honor opt-outs right away. Rules differ by message type and state, so have a lawyer or your compliance contact check your wording. I'm not giving legal advice here.

Also decide what happens when a number changes hands or a customer says "stop" by phone or email. Your list should update in one place.

## Setting it up in practice

1. List your members, their visit dates and renewal dates in one system.
2. Write the six messages above and have them approved by the owner.
3. Turn on the schedule for a small group first, such as one month's renewals.
4. Read every reply for the first few weeks.
5. Fix what confuses people, then widen the group.

## FAQ

**Can AI send maintenance agreement reminders for me?**
Yes, if you have a clear schedule and consent to message the customer. The AI drafts and sends on your timing, and a person handles every reply that isn't a simple yes.

**How many reminders should a member get?**
I'd start with a welcome, two or three visit notices, a thank-you after the visit and a renewal heads-up with one follow-up. Watch replies and opt-outs, then adjust.

**Should AI handle membership cancellations?**
No. Stop the reminders right away and have a person respond. A cancellation is a customer-relationship moment and may involve billing questions that need someone with account access.

**Do I need permission to text maintenance reminders?**
Generally yes. Get consent, include a way to opt out and honor it quickly. Because rules vary, have a lawyer review your wording and process.

**Can AI renew agreements on its own?**
I wouldn't let it change terms, charges or payment details without a person. It can remind, answer simple scheduling questions and hand off the rest.
