# AI for HVAC contractors: where it helps and where to start

**Meta description:** AI for HVAC contractors: what it does well on calls, estimates, follow-up, memberships, load calcs and dispatch notes, and the one place I'd start.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-for-hvac-contractors/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

AI for HVAC contractors means software that handles the repetitive, paper-heavy parts of the business: answering calls the office can't, drafting estimates from photos, following up on proposals, and writing up job notes. It does not replace a tech, a comfort advisor or a load calculation you can defend. Start with one job, such as missed calls or estimate follow-up, keep a person checking anything that touches money, scope or safety, and measure it for 30 days.

I run an HVAC company on Long Island and in New York City, and I build the AI we use. This is the HVAC-specific view. For the broader picture across trades, read [what AI actually does for the trades](/ai-for-the-trades/).

## Calls

HVAC is a business where the phone rings at the worst time. Someone has no heat, the office is on another line, and the caller moves to the next company on the list.

At Home+ we use an AI phone agent for calls the office can't answer. It collects the problem, address and callback number, asks a few triage questions, and creates a record in ServiceTitan. It does not diagnose, quote or promise arrival times. Gas smells and carbon monoxide alarms go to a person. I wrote up the setup in [how to answer every missed call with AI](/missed-calls-ai/), and there's a one-page checklist at [/missed-call-fix/](/missed-call-fix/).

## Estimates

This is where the time goes. A tech takes photos, then someone has to turn them into a proposal.

Our estimate copilot drafts a proposal from the tech's photos using our own price book. A person checks every one before it goes out. The AI never invents a price, because it only picks from lines we've already set. The details are in [AI estimates: from photos to proposal](/ai-estimates/). If you're wondering whether plain ChatGPT can do this, I cover where it stops in [ChatGPT for contractors](/chatgpt-for-contractors/).

## Follow-up

Most unsold estimates don't die because the customer said no. They die because nobody called back. A follow-up sequence that sounds like you and stops when the customer replies is one of the easiest wins. I lay out the cadence in [AI follow-up for unsold HVAC estimates](/ai-estimate-follow-up/).

## Maintenance agreements

Memberships are a paperwork problem as much as a sales one. We sign proposals and memberships on a phone, so there's no printing and no chasing a signature later. AI helps with the reminders around that: renewal notices, seasonal tune-up outreach and a clean record of who is on a plan. The pricing and terms stay yours. AI doesn't decide what a membership includes.

## Load calculations

Sizing equipment by rule of thumb is how you get a system that's too big or too small. At Home+ we scan a home with an iPad LiDAR, build a floor plan, and run a Manual J load calculation from it. The scan saves measuring time. It doesn't remove the need for a person who understands the house to check the inputs, such as insulation, windows and orientation. I cover that workflow in [LiDAR and Manual J](/lidar-manual-j/).

## Dispatch and job notes

This one is quiet but useful. A tech can talk through what they found, and AI turns it into a clean note on the job: what was done, what was recommended, what the customer declined. Dispatchers and the next tech get a clear history instead of a one-word summary. Treat these as drafts the tech reads and corrects, because the note becomes the record.

## Where I'd start

Pick one:

1. **Missed calls**, if you can see leads leaking on nights, weekends and busy hours.
2. **Estimate follow-up**, if you quote plenty but hear back from few.
3. **Estimate drafting**, if your best people are stuck at a desk writing proposals.

Count the problem yourself first. Pull two weeks of phone logs or a list of open estimates. Then run a 30 to 60 day pilot on that one workflow. If you're comparing vendors, use the questions in [how I'd pick an AI partner](/ai-partner-questions/). For a cost picture, see [what AI costs an HVAC company](/ai-cost-for-hvac-companies/), and for tools sorted by job, [AI tools for HVAC owners](/ai-tools-for-hvac-owners/).

## The rule I keep

A person checks anything that touches money, scope or safety. Prices, what's included in the job, what we tell a customer about a gas smell, a load calculation: a human signs off. AI does the first draft and the paperwork. That rule is why I'm comfortable running it on my own customers.

## What AI won't do

- Fix a bad process. If estimates sit for a week, AI drafts them faster into the same pile.
- Replace a good tech or comfort advisor. It supports them.
- Make your price book. You still decide your pricing.

## FAQ

**What is the best way for an HVAC contractor to start with AI?**
Pick one problem you can count, like missed calls or unfollowed estimates, and run a 30 to 60 day pilot on it. Keep a person reviewing anything about price, scope or safety.

**Can AI do an HVAC load calculation?**
It can speed up the measuring and data entry. At Home+ we scan with an iPad LiDAR, build a floor plan and run Manual J from it. A person still checks the inputs and the result.

**Does AI work with ServiceTitan?**
It can when the tools are connected to your account. Our jobs live in ServiceTitan, and ask any vendor to show a real call or estimate becoming a real record before you commit.

**Will AI answer my phones during emergencies?**
It can collect details and alert a person, but it should not handle safety emergencies alone. Gas smells, carbon monoxide alarms and flooding should always reach a human.

**Is ChatGPT enough for an HVAC company?**
For writing and brainstorming, often yes. For calls, estimates from your price book and updating your job system, you need tools connected to your systems.
