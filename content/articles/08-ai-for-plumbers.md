# How to use AI in a plumbing business (from an HVAC owner)

**Meta description:** How to use AI in a plumbing business: calls, price books and photo estimates for water heaters and repipes, what carries over from HVAC, and what doesn't.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-for-plumbers/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

To use AI in a plumbing business, start with the phone: send calls your office can't answer to an AI phone agent that collects the problem, address and callback number and flags emergencies for a person. Then use it for photo-based estimates from your own flat-rate price book and for follow-up on quotes that go quiet. A person should check every price and every safety call.

I should be upfront: I run an HVAC company, not a plumbing shop. What I know firsthand is how we use AI at Home+ Air & Heat. Techeo, which I founded, builds AI and marketing for HVAC and plumbing companies, so I've thought hard about what carries over. Here's my honest split.

## What carries over

### Emergency-heavy calls

Plumbing calls are often urgent. A burst pipe, no hot water or a sewer backup doesn't wait until Monday. That makes the phone the first place I'd put AI.

The setup is the same one I described in [how to answer every missed call with AI](/missed-calls-ai/). Count your missed calls, decide what the agent may and may not do, connect it to your job system, test it with ugly calls, and review recordings weekly. Only the triage questions change. Instead of no heat or no cool, you ask about active leaks, whether the water is shut off, no hot water, backups and whether anyone can reach the main valve.

The agent should not diagnose and should not promise an arrival time it can't see. Flooding and anything that sounds dangerous goes straight to a person. For a one-page version, see [/missed-call-fix/](/missed-call-fix/).

### Flat-rate price books

Plumbers often quote flat-rate: a set price for a defined job. That's well suited to AI, because the AI picks from lines you already wrote rather than making up numbers.

That's how our estimate copilot works at Home+. It drafts a proposal from the tech's photos using our own price book, and a person checks every one. An AI without your price book is guessing, so insist on that connection. Read [AI estimates: from photos to proposal](/ai-estimates/) for the mechanics.

### Photo estimates for water heaters and repipes

A photo of a water heater tells you a lot: the type, the venting, the age marks on the label, the space around it, the condition of the shutoffs. A tech can capture that in a minute, and AI can turn those photos into a draft: which option to offer, what accessories belong, what's included.

Repipes are different in scale but similar in shape. Photos of the existing pipe, the panel and the access points feed a draft scope. That's my reasoning from how our HVAC process works, not a result I've measured in a plumbing shop. Treat it as something to pilot.

### Follow-up

A water heater quote that goes quiet is a customer with no hot water thinking it over. The same follow-up that works for HVAC applies: a short note in the owner's voice, a couple of check-ins, and an instant stop when they reply. See [AI follow-up for unsold HVAC estimates](/ai-estimate-follow-up/) and swap in your own jobs.

## What's different

- **Scope surprises.** Behind a wall, pipe can be different from what the photo shows. Make the AI's draft say what's assumed, and have a person confirm scope before the customer signs.
- **Permits and code.** Rules vary by town. AI should not decide what the code requires. Have your people set that.
- **Safety wording.** Gas lines, flooding and electrical hazards near water need a human and a short, carefully written script you author.
- **Your trade's vocabulary.** Test any tool on your actual calls and jargon. What works for no-cool calls might trip over a drain stoppage.

## Where I'd start

1. Count missed calls for two weeks.
2. Put an AI phone agent on after-hours and overflow only.
3. Review every recording for the first two weeks.
4. Once that holds, try photo estimates on one job type, such as water heaters.
5. Add follow-up on open quotes.

If you're comparing partners, use [the questions I'd ask](/ai-partner-questions/). The full picture across trades is in [AI for the trades](/ai-for-the-trades/), and if you want to see where ChatGPT stops, read [ChatGPT for contractors](/chatgpt-for-contractors/).

## What not to hand to AI

- Final price on a custom job
- Anything about code, permits or safety
- Warranty promises
- Refund or dispute decisions

## FAQ

**Can AI answer phones for a plumbing company?**
Yes, for intake. It can collect the problem, address and callback number, ask triage questions and alert a person for emergencies. It shouldn't diagnose or quote.

**Can AI write a plumbing estimate from photos?**
It can draft one when it's connected to your price book, and that's the setup I use in HVAC. A person should verify scope and price before it goes to the customer.

**Do you run a plumbing company?**
No. I run an HVAC company. Techeo, my AI and marketing company, works with HVAC and plumbing companies, and the principles here are the ones I use at Home+.

**Is flat-rate pricing good for AI?**
It helps a lot, because the AI chooses from set prices instead of inventing numbers. Make sure the tool actually uses your price book.

**What should a plumber not automate?**
Final pricing on custom work, code or permit decisions, safety advice beyond directing callers to emergency steps, and warranty promises.
