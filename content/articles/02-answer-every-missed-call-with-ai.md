# How to answer every missed call in an HVAC or plumbing shop with AI

**Meta description:** How to answer every missed call in an HVAC or plumbing shop with an AI phone agent: setup steps, what to script, what to keep human, and mistakes to avoid.
**Suggested URL:** /missed-calls-ai/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

You answer every missed call by sending the ones your office can't pick up to an AI phone agent that collects the problem, address and callback number, gets it into your job system, and flags emergencies for a person. Set it up on after-hours, overflow and busy-line calls first, give it a short script and strict limits, and listen to its recordings weekly. It should book or hand off, not diagnose or improvise prices.

I run an HVAC company on Long Island, and we use this ourselves. Here's how I'd set it up if I were starting from zero.

## Step 1: Find out how many calls you actually miss

Before you buy anything, pull your phone log for the last two weeks. Sort calls into: answered, missed during hours, missed after hours, and voicemail. Note which missed numbers called back later and which didn't. You can't recover calls you never counted, and the log tells you where the leak is: after hours, lunch, or when the office is on the line with another customer.

If you don't have a log, forward your main number to a service that records call history, or check your carrier portal. This takes an hour and saves you from buying the wrong thing.

## Step 2: Decide what the AI is allowed to do

Write three lists:

- **It may do:** greet, ask what's wrong, collect name, address and callback number, ask a few triage questions (no heat, no cool, leak, smell, noise), offer a callback window, send a text confirmation.
- **It may not do:** diagnose, promise arrival times it can't see, quote repair or install prices, discuss warranty or rebate eligibility.
- **It must hand off:** safety words (gas smell, carbon monoxide alarm, smoke, flooding), angry customers, anyone who asks for a person.

For safety calls, the script should tell the caller to follow the right emergency steps and then alert a human. Write that language yourself, not from a template.

## Step 3: Connect it to where jobs live

The point isn't a nice voicemail. The point is a lead in your system. The agent should create a record in your field service software (for us that's ServiceTitan) with the transcript, number and address, or at least send a text and email to the person on call. If it can read your real schedule, it can offer real appointment slots. If it can't, don't let it invent availability: have it promise a callback.

## Step 4: Write a short opening and test it with bad calls

Keep the greeting under ten seconds. Say it's an automated assistant for your company, then ask how you can help. Then make 20 test calls to your own line, deliberately difficult: a mumbled address, a caller who talks over the agent, a wrong number, a customer asking about an existing job, an emergency, a spam call. Write down every place it fumbles and fix the script.

## Step 5: Start narrow

Turn it on for **after-hours and overflow only.** Let it run for two weeks while a person reviews every call recording each morning. Move it to during-hours overflow once you trust it. Don't start by routing every call through it.

## Step 6: Review weekly

Every week, open ten recordings. Look for: wrong addresses, callers who hung up, things the agent said that you don't want said, and calls it should have handed off. Adjust the script. Keep a short change log so you know what you changed and why.

## What it won't fix

- A slow callback. If the lead sits for six hours after the AI books it, you've only moved the problem.
- An office that doesn't follow up. The agent hands you a lead, it doesn't close one.
- Bad intake rules. If your team can't agree on what counts as an emergency, the agent can't either.

## Mistakes I see

- **Letting it quote prices.** It can't see your pricing logic, and a wrong number on a recording is a customer problem.
- **No human handoff.** A caller stuck in a loop will leave and tell people.
- **Not disclosing it's an AI.** Be upfront in the greeting. It's honest and avoids surprises.
- **Judging it by vendor demos.** Test it with your real calls and your real accents and background noise.
- **Skipping call-recording laws.** Check recording and consent rules for your state, and have your attorney review your disclosure language.

## Plumbers and other trades

The setup is identical: count the misses, set limits, connect to the job system, test with hard calls. Only the triage questions change: burst pipe, no hot water, sewer backup, and the emergency keywords differ.

## FAQ

**Can AI really answer calls for an HVAC company?**
Yes, for intake: it can collect the problem, address and callback and hand the lead to your team. It should not diagnose, quote prices it can't verify or handle safety emergencies alone.

**Is an AI answering service better than a human answering service?**
It depends on volume and complexity. AI is available at any hour and can write straight into your job system, while a human service handles odd situations better. Many shops use AI for overflow and keep a person for emergencies.

**Will customers hang up on an AI?**
Some will. Keep the greeting short, be upfront that it's automated, make it easy to ask for a person, and review recordings to find where callers drop.

**What should I tell the AI never to say?**
Prices, arrival times it can't see, diagnoses, warranty or rebate promises, and anything about safety beyond directing the caller to the right emergency steps.

**Does it work with ServiceTitan?**
It can if the agent is connected to your account through its tools or integrations. Ask any vendor to show a real call becoming a real record before you sign.
