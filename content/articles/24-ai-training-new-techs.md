# Using AI to train new HVAC technicians

**Meta description:** Using AI to train new HVAC technicians: an internal assistant that answers from your own SOPs and manuals, ride-along notes into checklists, quizzes, and the limits.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-training-new-techs/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

AI can help train new HVAC technicians by answering questions from your own procedures and manufacturer manuals, turning ride-along notes into checklists and quizzing people on what they've learned. It doesn't replace hands-on training or a senior tech, and it shouldn't be the source for safety procedures. Those come from the manufacturer's documents and your trainer. This is the approach I would take and recommend. It's not a description of a finished program at my company.

I run Home+ Air & Heat, and I build AI tools for trades companies at Techeo. New techs ask the same questions again and again, and the people who can answer them are usually on a job. That's the gap AI can fill, with limits. For my view on where AI stops and techs begin, see [will AI replace HVAC techs](/will-ai-replace-hvac-techs/).

## The three jobs AI can do

### 1. An internal assistant that answers from your own documents

Instead of asking a general chatbot, set up an assistant that only answers from material you give it: your SOPs, your job checklists, your price book terms, your call and booking procedures and the manufacturer manuals for the equipment you install and service.

Rules for it:

- **Answer only from the supplied documents.** If the answer isn't there, it says so and points to a person.
- **Show where the answer came from.** A new tech should see the document and page, so they can open it and read the source.
- **Keep it current.** When a manual or procedure changes, replace the old file. An assistant that quotes an outdated document is worse than none.
- **Control who sees what.** Don't load customer records, pay information or anything private.

This kind of tool works well for questions like where a form goes, what to photograph at a job, how to fill out notes or what a manufacturer's startup checklist includes. It's the old binder and the old "ask the senior guy," faster to search.

### 2. Ride-along notes turned into checklists

New techs ride along with experienced ones. The learning is real, but it's also forgotten, because nobody writes it down. Have the trainee take quick notes or voice memos after each job: what they saw, what the senior tech did, what they didn't understand.

Then ask AI to organize those notes into a checklist for that job type.

**Prompt to try:**
> Turn these ride-along notes into a step checklist for a new technician. Use only the steps in the notes. Mark anything unclear as "ask trainer." Do not add technical steps that aren't in the notes: `[notes]`

The senior tech reviews the checklist and corrects it. Over time you build a set of checklists that reflect how your company actually works, written by the people learning it. That's worth more than a generic template.

### 3. Quizzes and practice questions

AI is good at writing questions from material you give it. Paste in a section of your SOP or a manufacturer's guide and ask for ten questions with the answer and the page it came from. Use them for short weekly check-ins. Wrong answers tell your trainer where to spend time.

Have the trainer check every question and answer against the source. If the source is unclear, it's the trainer's call.

## Limits you should set

- **It doesn't replace hands-on training.** Brazing, gauges, wiring and troubleshooting are learned by doing, with someone watching. A chat window can't see a trainee's hands.
- **It doesn't replace a senior tech.** Judgment comes from watching someone good handle a bad day. Pair every new tech with a person.
- **Safety comes from the manufacturer and your trainer.** Electrical, gas, refrigerant and combustion procedures should come from the manufacturer's instructions, applicable codes, your training and your trainer. The assistant can point to them. It shouldn't be where anyone learns them or the last word on them.
- **It can be wrong.** AI sounds sure even when it's mistaken. Tell trainees to confirm anything that affects safety or the customer's system with their trainer before acting.
- **It doesn't certify anyone.** Whether someone is ready for a task is a person's decision.

## How I would roll it out

1. Pick one area, such as paperwork and job notes, where mistakes are cheap.
2. Collect the 10 to 20 documents people ask about most and load them.
3. Have a senior tech test it with 20 real questions and mark which answers are right.
4. Let one new hire use it, with their trainer reading the questions asked.
5. Fix gaps by improving the documents, not by trusting the AI more.

The questions new techs ask are useful in themselves. If five people ask the same thing, your documents or your onboarding are missing something. If you're choosing a tool for this, the questions in [how I'd pick an AI partner](/ai-partner-questions/) apply here too.

## What this does for the team

Used well, it gives new techs a faster way to find answers, gives your senior techs fewer interruptions and builds a written record of how you do things. It does not make the training shorter. It makes the questions better. For more on where AI fits across a shop, see [AI for HVAC contractors](/ai-for-hvac-contractors/).

## FAQ

**Can AI train a new HVAC technician?**
Not by itself. It can answer questions from your documents, build checklists from ride-along notes and quiz people. Skills and judgment still come from hands-on work with a senior tech.

**Can I load manufacturer manuals into an AI assistant?**
Check each manufacturer's terms for how their documents may be used and shared. Where it's allowed, load them so the assistant can point to the source pages, and always send safety questions back to the manual and your trainer.

**Is it safe to rely on AI for refrigerant, gas or electrical procedures?**
No. Those procedures come from manufacturer instructions, applicable codes and your trainer. AI can help someone find the right document, but it shouldn't be the authority.

**How do I stop the assistant from making things up?**
Limit it to your documents, require it to name the source and say "I don't know" when the answer isn't there. Then test it with real questions and have a senior tech check the answers.

**What should I not put into a training assistant?**
Customer names and addresses, pay information, and anything private. Use procedures, checklists and equipment documents only.
