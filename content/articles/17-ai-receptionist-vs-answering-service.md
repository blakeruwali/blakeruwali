# AI receptionist vs answering service for contractors: which one fits

**Meta description:** AI receptionist vs answering service for contractors: how a human service, an AI phone agent and a hybrid compare, when each fits, and what to ask before you sign.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

A human answering service gives you a live person who takes a message, an AI receptionist gives you a phone agent that collects details and creates a job record, and a hybrid uses AI first and hands off to a person when it should. None is best for everyone. Pick based on your call volume, how many of your calls are emergencies, and whether the answer needs to land in your job system or just in your inbox.

I run an HVAC company, and I build the AI phone agent we use for calls the office can't answer. That's a bias, so I'll try to be fair. I've also built this, which means I know where it breaks. Setup steps are in [how to answer every missed call with AI](/missed-calls-ai/), so I won't repeat them here.

## What each option is

**A human answering service.** A call center answers on behalf of many businesses. A trained person picks up, follows your script, takes a message and sends it to you by text or email, or transfers urgent calls.

**An AI receptionist (phone agent).** Software answers, talks to the caller, asks the questions you've set, and records the result. In our shop it creates a record in ServiceTitan for a person to follow up. It does not diagnose, quote prices or promise arrival times.

**A hybrid.** The AI answers first. A person takes over for emergencies, upset callers, anything the AI can't handle, or any call the caller asks a human for. Some setups work the other way, with a human service as the backup when the AI is stuck.

## Where each one is strong

### Human answering service

- A real voice, which some callers prefer and some situations need.
- Handles the unexpected. A person can sense when a caller is scared or confused.
- Simple to understand: they take a message.
- Works without much setup on your side.

### AI receptionist

- Can answer many calls at once. After a storm or on the first hot day, the lines don't back up.
- Consistent. It asks the same questions the same way every time.
- Can write directly into your job system, so the details don't live in a text message someone has to retype.
- Every call has a transcript and a recording you can review.

### Hybrid

- The AI takes the routine volume, and people handle the calls that matter most.
- You decide which calls go to a person, such as anything about gas, carbon monoxide, flooding or fire.

## Where each one falls short

### Human answering service

- Messages often still have to be re-entered into your system.
- A service handling many businesses may not know your trade, your service area or your rules as well as you'd like. Ask how they're trained.
- When everyone's phone rings at once, such as during a storm, even a large service can have hold times.
- You pay for their staffing model, so check how pricing works when volume spikes.

### AI receptionist

- It can misunderstand accents, noise, or a caller who rambles.
- It needs tight limits. An AI left loose will improvise, and improvising on price or safety is a problem.
- Some callers hang up on a machine. Listen to recordings and see how many.
- It needs to connect to your systems to be worth much. A phone agent that only sends you a text is a fancy voicemail.

### Hybrid

- More moving parts. You need clear rules about when the AI hands off and who picks up.
- Someone has to be reachable. A handoff to a person who doesn't answer is worse than no handoff.

## When each one fits

1. **You get a few missed calls a week and a person is usually around.** A human service or a simple voicemail with a fast callback may be enough. Count first. Don't buy more than your problem needs.
2. **You miss many calls, especially after hours or at peak times, and your jobs live in a system like ServiceTitan.** An AI phone agent that writes into the system is worth testing.
3. **You have a meaningful share of emergency calls.** Use a hybrid with an explicit human handoff for anything involving safety. I wouldn't let AI alone handle gas smells, carbon monoxide alarms or flooding.
4. **You want a person's voice on every call.** Use a human service and accept the trade-offs.

If you aren't sure, pull two weeks of phone logs and sort the calls: answered, missed during hours, missed after hours and voicemail. That list tells you which problem you have. There's a one-page version at [/missed-call-fix/](/missed-call-fix/).

## Questions to ask any provider

1. Can I hear recordings of real calls, including bad ones?
2. What does a call become in my system: a text, an email or a real job record?
3. Which calls go to a person, and how fast?
4. What can it say about price, scheduling and safety, and can I see those limits in writing?
5. Where are recordings and transcripts stored, who can see them, and how do you handle call-recording consent rules in my state?
6. How is it priced, and what does a worst-case month cost?
7. Can I pause it myself, and can I leave without losing my data?

## What I'd do

If I were starting over I'd run a 30-day pilot on after-hours and overflow calls only, with a human handoff for emergencies, and review the recordings every week. I'd compare it against my own phone log from before. That tells me more than any sales demo.

## FAQ

**Is an AI receptionist better than an answering service?**
Neither is better for everyone. AI scales and can write into your job system. A human service brings a live voice and judgment on odd calls. Many shops do best with a hybrid.

**Can an AI receptionist handle emergency calls?**
It can collect details and alert a person, but it shouldn't handle safety emergencies alone. Gas smells, carbon monoxide alarms and flooding should reach a human.

**Will customers hang up on an AI?**
Some will. Listen to your recordings and track how many callers drop off. If it's a problem, change the greeting or send more calls to a person.

**Does an answering service connect to my job system?**
It depends on the provider. Many send a text or email, which someone has to enter by hand. Ask to see a real call turn into a real record before you sign.

**What should I pilot first?**
After-hours and overflow calls, for 30 days, with a written list of what the AI may and may not say and a weekly review of recordings.
