# How to show up in ChatGPT and Google AI answers as a contractor

**Meta description:** How to show up in ChatGPT and Google AI answers as a contractor: no one can guarantee it, but consistent business info, real reviews and answer-first pages raise your odds.
**Suggested URL:** /ai-search-for-contractors/
**Byline:** Blake Ruwali, owner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo

Nobody can guarantee your company gets named in ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI answers, and anyone who sells that is guessing. What you can do is raise the odds: keep your business information the same everywhere, build a strong Google Business Profile, collect real reviews, publish clear service-area pages with answer-first FAQs, get mentioned on other sites and put useful videos on YouTube. That's the approach I'd take for any trades company, and none of it is a trick.

## How these tools pick what to say

AI search tools build an answer from two things. One is what the model already knows about businesses and topics. The other is pages and sources it looks up at the moment you ask. They change by question, by day and by person, so there is no "rank number one" to buy. It's closer to being the kind of source that's easy to find, easy to trust and easy to quote.

The research I've read, mostly from software vendors with different samples, points the same way. Forums like Reddit and video on YouTube get cited a lot. Reference sites like Wikipedia get used heavily by some tools. Clear, well-organized pages and consistent facts about a business beat tricks. Treat all of it as a rough direction, not a rulebook.

## What helps

### 1. Make your business info identical everywhere

Your name, address, phone number, website, hours and service area should match on your site, Google Business Profile, Facebook, Yelp, LinkedIn and any directory. If one place says "Air & Heat" and another says "Heating and Cooling," tools have to guess. Pick one version and fix the rest.

### 2. Fill out your Google Business Profile completely

Choose the right categories. List your services and service area. Add real photos of your work and team. Keep hours current. Reply to reviews. It's free, and it's one of the first places customers and search tools look.

### 3. Collect real reviews

Reviews tell people and tools that you're real and what working with you is like. Ask every customer the same way. Never buy, fake or write them. I cover the rules in [AI and Google reviews](/ai-google-reviews/).

### 4. Build clear service-area pages

Make one page per service and area you actually cover. Each should say what you do, where, how booking works and what the customer can expect. Don't copy the same page and swap the town name. If you don't work in a place, don't make a page for it.

### 5. Write answer-first FAQ pages

Put the direct answer in the first two sentences, then explain. Use the real questions customers ask on the phone, such as how a visit works, what a membership includes or what to do when a system stops working. Write them from real calls, not from a template. The articles on this site follow that format on purpose, for example [AI for HVAC contractors](/ai-for-hvac-contractors/).

### 6. Get mentioned on other sites

Third-party mentions carry weight. Local business groups, trade publications, supplier or manufacturer pages that list contractors, chamber of commerce listings, podcasts and community forums all count. Earn them with something useful: an honest answer, a real guest appearance, a local story.

On forums such as Reddit, read the rules first. Many trade communities remove marketing. Answer questions with first-hand detail, say who you are when it's relevant and don't drop links just to drop links.

### 7. Publish on YouTube

Video gets cited, and it also shows customers who you are. Use plain titles that match the question, such as how a visit works or what to check before calling for service. Put the answer in the first two lines of the description. Add a real transcript to the page on your site, not just auto-captions.

## What I wouldn't count on

- **Guarantees.** If someone promises a spot in AI answers, ask how they'd prove it and what happens if it doesn't happen.
- **`llms.txt` files.** It's a file meant to point AI tools to your content. I haven't seen solid evidence it changes whether you're named. It's cheap to publish, so I do, but I don't count on it.
- **Mass-produced pages.** Hundreds of near-identical town pages are the opposite of useful.
- **Fake reviews or fake mentions.** They break platform rules and trust, and they can get your listing removed.

## How to check where you stand

Once a month, ask the same set of 10 questions a customer might ask, in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google, such as "who installs mini splits in [your town]." Write down whether your company is named, whether your site is cited and what's wrong or missing. Don't expect the same answer twice. Look for the trend across months, and fix wrong information at the source.

## Where AI fits in doing this work

AI can help draft FAQ pages from call notes, tidy service descriptions and spot gaps in your site. It can't make a fact true. Before anything goes live, someone who knows the work checks it. ## FAQ

**Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?**
No. There's no placement you can buy in its answers. What you can do is make your business easy to find, consistent and well documented, then check how you appear over time.

**Does Google Business Profile matter for AI answers?**
It matters for customers and for search in general, and it's one of the main places your business information lives. Keep it complete and current. I can't tell you exactly how much weight each AI tool gives it.

**Do I need an llms.txt file?**
It's not required, and I haven't seen proof that it changes whether you're named. It's quick and harmless to add, but spend your time on real pages, reviews and mentions first.

**How long does it take to show up?**
There's no fixed timeline, and some tools may never name a small local company. Work on the basics, track the same questions monthly and judge by what you see, not by promises.

**Should a small contractor bother with YouTube?**
If you can record a few honest, useful videos from real work, yes. Keep them simple, use plain titles and put the answer early. It helps customers find you as well as search tools.
