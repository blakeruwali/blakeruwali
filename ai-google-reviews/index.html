Using AI to get and reply to Google reviews as a contractor

Using AI to get and reply to Google reviews for contractors: ask every customer the same way, draft replies a person edits, and never fake, gate, incentivize or AI-write reviews.

Blake RuwaliOwner of Home+ Air & Heat and founder of Techeo5 min read · Nov 14, 2026

AI can help a contractor with Google reviews in two ways: it can make sure every customer is asked, and it can draft replies for a person to edit. It must never write the reviews themselves, buy them or hide who is being asked. Ask every customer the same way, don't only ask the ones who seem happy, don't offer rewards for reviews, and keep customer details out of your replies.

I run Home+ Air & Heat, so I read our reviews and reply to them. This is how I'd use AI around them, and the lines I wouldn't cross.

Why reviews matter

Customers read reviews before they call, and search tools use them as one signal that a business is real. A steady flow of honest reviews does more for you than a burst of perfect ones. That's why the process should be boring and consistent, not clever. I cover how reviews fit with being found by AI tools in AI search for contractors.

Part 1: Asking for reviews

Ask every customer the same way

The simple rule: every finished job gets the same request, sent the same way, at the same point. Don't screen customers first. Google's policies prohibit review gating, which means only asking happy customers or steering unhappy ones elsewhere. A fair process asks everyone, then lets the reviews fall where they fall.

A good time to ask is shortly after the job is done and the customer has a working system. Make it easy: one text or email with a direct link to your Google review page.

Example message:

Hi, it's Blake from Home+. Thanks for having us out. If you have a minute, an honest review on Google helps other homeowners. Here's the link. Thank you either way.

Don't write "5 stars" in the request. Don't hint at what they should say.

What AI does here

  • Drafts and rotates a few versions of the request message so they sound natural.
  • Triggers the message when a job is marked complete in your system.
  • Sends one reminder, not five.
  • Flags customers who replied with a problem so a person can call before anything else goes out.

That last point is fine as long as everyone still gets the same review request. Calling an upset customer to fix a problem is good service. Skipping the request for them, or sending them to a private form instead of Google, is gating.

What not to do

  • No incentives. Don't offer discounts, gifts or entries in a drawing for reviews.
  • No fake reviews. Not from staff, friends or relatives pretending to be customers.
  • No AI-written reviews. Not even "templates" you hand to customers to paste in. A review should be the customer's own words.
  • No review swaps with other businesses.
  • No gating. See above.

Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your listing penalized, and it erodes the trust you're trying to earn.

Part 2: Replying to reviews

Reply to every review, good and bad. It shows future customers how you treat people. AI is good at the first draft, because replies are repetitive and short.

A workflow

  1. Paste the review text into your AI tool, without the reviewer's last name or address if they're in the text.
  2. Ask for a short, warm reply in your voice.
  3. Edit it so it sounds like you and mentions something specific from the review.
  4. A person approves and posts it.

Prompt to try:

Draft a 40-word reply to this Google review for a small HVAC company. Warm, plain language, no promises, no discounts, no details about the job beyond what the reviewer wrote. Review: [paste review]

Replies to positive reviews

Thank them, mention one thing they said and keep it short. Rotating a few phrases is fine. Posting the same sentence under fifty reviews isn't.

Replies to negative reviews

This is where a person matters most. AI can draft a calm first version. You read it, check the facts internally and decide what to offer. Then reply publicly with something like an apology for the experience and an invitation to contact the office, and take the details offline. Don't argue, don't blame the customer and don't promise a fix you haven't cleared.

Protect customer privacy

Don't reveal anything about the job in a public reply. That means no address, no description of what was wrong with their system, no price, no schedule details and no account information. Even if the customer shared them in the review, keep your reply general. The AI will happily repeat details it was given, so strip them out before you paste and read before you post.

What a person should always do

  • Approve every reply before it posts.
  • Handle every negative review personally.
  • Check that the review asks match your policy for all customers.
  • Report fake or abusive reviews through Google's tools, rather than answering them as if they were real.

A small checklist

  1. Same review request for every completed job.
  2. No rewards, no screening, no scripts.
  3. Replies drafted by AI, edited and posted by a person.
  4. No customer details in public replies.
  5. A monthly look at what reviews say, so you can fix real problems.

That last step is where the value is. AI can summarize themes across your reviews, such as scheduling, communication or cleanup. The fixes still come from you. For more on using AI for customer-facing writing, see ChatGPT for contractors and AI marketing for HVAC contractors.

FAQ

Can I use AI to write Google reviews for my business? No. Reviews must come from real customers in their own words. Writing them yourself, with or without AI, is fake review activity and can get them removed.

Is it okay to only ask happy customers for reviews? No. That's review gating, which Google's policies prohibit. Ask every customer the same way and let the reviews reflect real experiences.

Can I give customers a discount for leaving a review? No. Don't offer anything in exchange for a review. Say thank you and make the request easy instead.

Can AI reply to my Google reviews automatically? I wouldn't let it post without a person. Let it draft, then edit and approve each reply, especially negative ones, so nothing wrong or private goes public.

What should I never put in a review reply? Anything that identifies the customer or their job: address, equipment problems, prices, schedule or account details. Keep replies short, polite and general.
